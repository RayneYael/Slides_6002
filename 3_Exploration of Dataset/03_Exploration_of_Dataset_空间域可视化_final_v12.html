EXPLORATION OF DATASET  ·  SCALE VS INTENSITY

Total emissions and per-capita emissions tell different stories.

Land colour shows total CO₂ on a logarithmic scale; 3-D bar height and colour show CO₂ per person. Together they contrast national scale with emissions intensity.

KEY POINTS
  • Two extremes — China leads total emissions, while Qatar has the highest per-capita bar.
  • Scale contrast — large economies can dominate the total without ranking highest per person; small countries can show the reverse.
  • Implication — total and per-capita emissions must be read together when comparing countries.

SLIDE NOTES · EDITABLE

This map deliberately combines two complementary measures. Land fill represents total CO₂ on a log₁₀ megatonne scale, while 3-D bar height and colour represent CO₂ per person in tonnes per person. The paired encodings reveal the contrast between national scale and emissions intensity: China leads total emissions, whereas Qatar has the highest per-capita value. Natural Earth 110m polygons match 169 of the 213 panel entities but still cover approximately 99.6% of global emissions. Some small islands and territories may therefore be absent from land fill; selected small-country bars are positioned separately. This categorical choropleth shows each country's dominant fossil-fuel source in 2021: coal, oil or gas. Pie charts provide the actual three-fuel shares for selected major emitters, preventing the dominant-category map from hiding mixed national profiles. The figure is intended to compare spatial regimes and fuel structure, not the magnitude of total emissions.
