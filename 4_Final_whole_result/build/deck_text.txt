COHORT FUNNEL
6,378
country-year rows | OWID backbone + GCB fuel fills, 1992-2021
213
countries & territories | valid ISO codes in the panel
-16
micro states excluded | populations too small for stable trajectories
-4
incomplete histories excluded | fewer than 30 consecutive years 1992-2021
193
modelled countries / entities | complete history + reliable fuel structure
WHY THIS COHORT
Trajectory features need complete histories. Slopes, peaks and post-peak change are only comparable when every country covers the same 30 years. | Fuel features need reliable shares. Entities with unreliable fuel structure would distort the fuel-shift block.
ONE COUNTRY = ONE PROFILE | Each of the 193 countries becomes a single 11-feature trajectory profile - the clustering unit for everything that follows.
AUDITABLE BY DESIGN | Every exclusion is logged with its reason in cohort_audit.csv, so the sample can be reproduced or challenged.
===== SLIDE 12 =====
DESIGN OF AI ALGORITHM · Turning 30-year emission histories into model features | Eleven features describe level, direction, timing and fuel shift
2 / 4
Responsible: RAN CHANGMING
12
01 · LEVEL
How intense is the economy now?
· Log recent per-capita CO2 | · (median 2017-2021)
Log scale stops extreme values from dominating distances.
02 · DIRECTION
Which way is the trajectory moving?
· Long-term log slope 1992-2021 | · Recent log slope 2012-2021 | · Acceleration (recent - long-term)
Separates long-run direction from recent slowdown or renewed growth.
03 · TIMING
When does change happen?
· Growth volatility (MAD) | · Peak timing (relative) | · Post-peak change
Distinguishes smooth plateauing from unstable or still-rising paths.
04 · FUEL SHIFT
Is the fuel mix decarbonising?
· Recent coal & gas shares | · Coal & gas share change | · (early vs recent 5-yr medians)
Oil share is kept for interpretation but omitted from the model to avoid perfect dependence.
Built only from per-capita CO2, growth rates and fuel shares - total CO2 and population never enter the model; they return later, only to interpret the pathways.
===== SLIDE 13 =====
DESIGN OF AI ALGORITHM · Scaling, PCA, K-means and how they are validated | A fixed pipeline turns 30-year histories into two pathways
3 / 4
Responsible: RAN CHANGMING
[4]
13
COUNTRY-YEAR PANEL
6,378 rows · 1992-2021
193 COUNTRY PROFILES
one row per country, 11 features
ROBUSTSCALER
median / IQR scaling, so outliers cannot dominate distances
PCA → 4 COMPONENTS
87.41% of variance kept, correlated features merged
K-MEANS · K = 2-8
50 restarts, fixed seed 42: the grouping re-runs identically
VALIDATION RUNS ALONGSIDE - NOT INSIDE - THE CLUSTERING
GMM SOFT MEMBERSHIP | probability of belonging to each cluster
100 x 80% SUBSAMPLING | refit and compare labels (Adjusted Rand Index)
NEITHER CHANGES THE LABELS | both instruments only test the grouping that K-means already produced
WHY K-MEANS
Unsupervised by design: no labels exist for 'transition pathway', so we cluster comparable profiles and validate the structure afterwards. | Simple, explainable, reproducible: a fixed seed and n_init = 50 make the exact grouping re-runnable; PCA removes redundancy among correlated trajectory features before distances are computed.

DESIGN OF AI ALGORITHM · Choosing how many emission patterns to keep (K = 2-8) | All three validity metrics rank K = 2 first
4 / 4
Responsible: RAN CHANGMING
[4] [5]
14
Inertia (within-cluster sum of squares) always falls as K grows - shown for reference only, never used alone to pick K.
How the decision was made | · Three internal criteria were computed on the same PCA space for every candidate K from 2 to 8, so the comparison is like-for-like. | · All three agree on K = 2; no criterion prefers a finer split, and K = 3-8 lose separation without adding interpretable structure. | · K = 2 is therefore a screening frame for monitoring attention, not a claim that only two kinds of country exist.

highest - strongest split
DECISION | K = 2 - the strongest macro segmentation, not the most detailed taxonomy.

Highlighted row: K = 2 wins Silhouette, Davies-Bouldin and Calinski-Harabasz simultaneously.
SILHOUETTE | Rewards clusters that are internally compact and well separated from neighbours.
DAVIES-BOULDIN | Penalises overlap between cluster pairs; lower means cleaner separation.
CALINSKI-HARABASZ | Compares between-cluster with within-cluster dispersion; higher is better.
NO SUPERVISED ACCURACY | Accuracy, precision, recall, F1, ROC and confusion matrices require ground-truth labels. Pathways have none - evaluation therefore uses internal validity plus stability.

===== SLIDE 22 =====
CONCLUSIONS · What this investigation changes for climate monitoring | From one emission total to pathway-specific monitoring
Responsible: GROUP 30 - ALL MEMBERS
22
CURRENT BURDEN
The 169-country plateauing pathway holds 92.2% of 2017-2021 sample emissions
Push the plateau into absolute decline - this is the short-term mitigation lever.
FUTURE PRESSURE
The 24-country rapid-growth pathway: fast growth, late peaks, +198% since 1992
Track growth and lock-in signals now, before today's growth becomes tomorrow's burden.
BOUNDARY UNCERTAINTY
10 low-confidence countries identified by soft membership
Observe them more frequently instead of forcing a fixed classification.
What is novel here: an 11-feature trajectory profile (level · direction · timing · fuel shift) that makes 30 years of history comparable per country, and a stability-checked K = 2 screen used as a monitoring tool - not as another league table.
Honest limits: pathways are empirical patterns in 1992-2021, not causes, forecasts or policy verdicts; with no policy or economic variables in the panel, the model prioritises deeper investigation - it does not replace it.
The purpose is not to rank countries, but to identify where different transition pressures require different monitoring priorities.
